### Intelligent Systems Final Project

**First, Pitch Your Idea**
**DUE: Thursday, October 29**

The learning goal of this project is for each student to 1) increase their knowledge of intelligent systems, as well as 2) acquire or improve a skill related to the design, construction, and/or analysis of an intelligent system.

This project is very open-ended, because I want students to pursue those aspects of intelligent systems that they are interested in. You will receive feedback as you propose, design, and develop your project to keep you on track and progressing towards a final product. There are many sites with interesting project ideas. It is okay to pick one and duplicate their project, provided you are not just copying and pasting code. Remember that the whole point is to learn something. I will let you know if I think the project is too small (or too big).

Your project needs to incorporate a small-scale processor (like the Metro board) and sensing of the environment. If you want moving parts, I can help you pick out motors and develop motor control. You might choose something more software focused, such as using machine learning techniques to create a voice recognition system; or more hardware focused, such as a wearable device for monitoring your heart rate. You might take advantage of the Maker Space in the Physics Department with free 3D printing. And there are many, many, many modules (i.e. breakout boards) that are designed to work with Arduino (https://www.adafruit.com/category/42). Note that if I am buying the hardware, there are budgetary constraints, but the majority are very inexpensive.

<hr>

First, everyone will individually pitch a project. Next Thursday, October 29 each student will have 5 minutes to present to the class an idea that they have. This pitch should include an overview of the project, learning objectives, and hardware requirements. Be as specific as possible. Think about the descriptions that you get at the start of a programming assignment that has the real-world application and the components of the prototype.

Also, let others know if you are looking for a team to complete the project. If you already have a project and partner in mind, each of you still needs to present independently, because you should have different aspects of the project to work on with distinct learning objectives.

_This does not have to be your final project. You can change your mind or jump on board with another team._

Afterwards, each person will complete a Google form related to the pitched projects. This will give you an opportunity to share your preferences for a project and team members.

<hr>

**__WHO__**: This project can be completed individually or in teams of 2 or 3. The project must scale with team size and each person’s role and learning objectives must be clearly defined. I strongly prefer that students work in a team, but I am aware of the complexity and potential unfairness of team projects.

**The teams will need to be approved by Dr. Larson.** I have high expectations for being a good team member, which means that you show up for meetings, you stay in communication, and you do the work that others rely on you to do. As I consider approving a student to be part of a team, I will take into consideration their work throughout the semester, including attendance and assignment submissions.

**__WHY__**: You motivate the work by identifying your learning objectives and outcomes. A learning objective is a high level description of the understanding and skills that you want to achieve. The learning outcomes are the concrete, actionable, and typically measurable skills that students will acquire after completing the project.

For example, a learning objective might be to understand the practical constraints of implementing machine learning using an artificial neural network (ANN) on a microcontroller. Some learning outcomes would be related to the implementation, for example, implement an ANN on the Metro that is trained for voice recognition, and interface the Metro with a microphone amplifier module. Some learning outcomes might be related to analyzing this system, for example, state the performance impact with respect to accuracy and runtime efficiency of ANN configurations that scale in complexity.

The site below discusses how to develop learning goals, objectives, and outcomes. It is written for instructors, so that the activities of a course or project are in service to those objectives and desired student outcomes.

https://resources.depaul.edu/teaching-commons/teaching-guides/course-design/Pages/course-objectives-learning-outcomes.aspx

**__WHAT__**: Primary Deliverables:

- Proposal that, most importantly, identifies learning objectives and anticipated learning outcomes related to Intelligent Systems. It will also provide the technical details of the project.

- Planning document structured similarly to the CDIO framework. This should put your project in the context of the real-world.

- Write-Up detailing both the anticipated and unanticipated learning outcomes with respect to the learning objectives. This will also detail the technical aspects of the project.

- Demonstration during our finals time. This will be a "science fair" format in which students and invited faculty will visit different stations to see what you have done.

- Periodic stand-ups in the final weeks of class. 

> I appreciate risk-taking that serves the purpose of learning. This is an academic environment and what better place is there to try those things that have unknown results! I am far more interested in what you learn than what you produce. 


### Hardware Specification

As part of the project proposal, you will need to determine what hardware you need. I would like you to find that hardware and propose purchasing it. Finding hardware can be difficult, particularly if you want it to be somewhat tested with documentation and support (and still be relatively inexpensive). _Amazon_ will suggest a lot of components that you can purchase, but many of them come from China, which is fine if you can read the datasheet written in Chinese (although AI can probably interpret for you). And as you know, even datasheets written in your native language require additional knowledge to interpret.
